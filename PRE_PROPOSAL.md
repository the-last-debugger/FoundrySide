# FoundrySide
## Comprehensive Pre-Proposal and Research Framework

**Working title:** FoundrySide — Autonomous Sports Intelligence and Prediction Research Ecosystem  
**Project status:** Conceptual research and feasibility assessment  
**Document purpose:** Establish the project's vision, objectives, scope, research priorities, constraints and expectations before preparing a formal project proposal.  
**Implementation status:** Not authorised. Research and technical investigation only.

---

# 1. Introduction

FoundrySide is envisioned as an autonomous sports intelligence and prediction research ecosystem designed to investigate sporting events, betting markets, probability estimation, statistical modelling and potential market inefficiencies.

The ambition is to develop a system capable of independently researching sporting events, generating predictions, evaluating its own performance, investigating failures, experimenting with alternative methods and continuously improving its predictive capabilities.

This is not intended to be another conventional betting tips website that publishes predictions without demonstrating how they were produced or whether they actually work.

FoundrySide should instead function as a continuously operating research laboratory. Its predictions should be measurable, its experiments reproducible, its historical records preserved, and its conclusions supported by evidence.

The system should eventually provide users with useful intelligence that helps them make better-informed decisions than they might make without it.

An important distinction is that improving a user's decision-making advantage does not necessarily mean beating bookmakers or consistently generating profits. A system can be valuable by providing better probability estimates, identifying uncertainty, exposing misleading odds, evaluating risk and helping users avoid poor decisions.

The project should investigate whether these advantages can be achieved across different sports, markets and betting scenarios.

# 2. The Central Vision

The central vision is to create an autonomous system that can:

1. Discover sporting events, competitions, fixtures, markets and available jackpots.
2. Collect relevant historical, current and contextual sporting data.
3. Retrieve and evaluate bookmaker odds and market information.
4. Develop independent probability estimates using statistical, mathematical and machine-learning approaches.
5. Compare its predictions against market-implied probabilities.
6. Identify possible differences between its estimates and the available market prices.
7. Experiment with different prediction methods and combinations of methods.
8. Evaluate predictions against actual outcomes.
9. Identify recurring weaknesses, unexplained errors and opportunities for improvement.
10. Preserve historical predictions, experiments, results and model versions.
11. Conduct further research without requiring the user to manually direct every experiment.
12. Communicate findings, confidence levels, limitations and discoveries in an understandable manner.

The system should be designed around continuous learning through controlled experimentation rather than assuming that a particular prediction methodology is already optimal.

The ambition is not simply to produce more predictions. It is to progressively develop better ways of understanding sporting events and estimating their outcomes.

# 3. FoundrySide as a Research Laboratory

FoundrySide should be treated as an evolving collection of independent predictive engines, experiments and research processes.

Different approaches should be allowed to compete.

For example, the system could investigate:

- Traditional statistical models.
- Machine-learning models.
- Historical performance and team-strength models.
- Contextual models incorporating injuries, schedules, motivation and other relevant factors.
- Market-derived probability models.
- Hybrid approaches combining multiple independent sources of information.
- Ensemble models that combine the outputs of several predictive engines.
- Experimental approaches that may initially appear unconventional.

We should not assume that one methodology will outperform all others.

A model that performs well in one sport, league, market or time period may perform poorly elsewhere. Likewise, a model that performs well historically may fail when conditions change.

FoundrySide should therefore investigate performance at different levels of classification, rather than relying exclusively on broad aggregate statistics.

The research environment should preserve competing approaches instead of prematurely eliminating methods that have not yet received sufficient testing.

## 3.1 Independent predictive engines

Where technically and economically feasible, different predictive engines should operate independently.

Each should have a clearly defined methodology, input requirements, output format and performance history.

Their predictions should be evaluated separately before being combined.

This would allow FoundrySide to investigate questions such as:

- Which methods perform best for particular markets?
- Which methods are more reliable in particular leagues?
- Are certain models better at identifying underdogs?
- Does combining otherwise mediocre models produce a stronger ensemble?
- Are some models useful only under particular conditions?
- Does a model's advantage disappear when tested on unseen data?

The central ensemble should not automatically trust the newest or most sophisticated model. Its composition should be determined by demonstrated performance.

## 3.2 Experimentation and model promotion

Every significant experiment should have a documented hypothesis, methodology, dataset, evaluation period and result.

New models should be tested against appropriate baselines and unseen data before being promoted.

Historical predictions and model versions should remain available even after a model is discontinued.

A failed experiment is still useful if its methodology and findings are preserved.

FoundrySide should avoid changing historical predictions retrospectively to make a model appear more successful.

# 4. The Market as a Reference Point, Not an Absolute Authority

One of the project's important research directions is the relationship between independent probability estimates and bookmaker markets.

Market odds contain information about how a betting market collectively prices an outcome. They are therefore an important reference point.

However, FoundrySide should not assume that the market is always correct, nor should it assume that every difference between an independent model and market odds represents a genuine opportunity.

The system should investigate whether its independent estimates can improve upon market-derived baselines under appropriately controlled conditions.

This investigation should include:

1. Comparing independent predictions with market-implied probabilities.
2. Accounting for bookmaker margins and overround.
3. Comparing performance across bookmakers and market types.
4. Investigating differences between opening and closing odds.
5. Measuring performance across different time intervals.
6. Investigating whether certain markets or competitions are consistently more difficult to price.
7. Examining whether market efficiency changes with liquidity, available information or other contextual variables.
8. Testing whether combining market information with independent models produces better results than either approach alone.

A particularly interesting research question is whether market performance varies over time.

For example, does a market behave differently several hours before kick-off compared with the final hour? Are there recurring periods when the relationship between market prices and actual outcomes changes?

These are hypotheses to investigate, not conclusions to assume.

The broader objective is to understand where market information is useful, where it is insufficient and whether independent methods can provide additional information.

FoundrySide should investigate multiple sports rather than being permanently restricted to football.

However, broad coverage should not come at the expense of prediction quality or operational sustainability.

The eventual coverage should be determined by data availability, predictive feasibility, cost and demonstrated performance.

Potential markets include:

- Correct score.

The research should establish which markets can be supported reliably and which require data that may be too expensive or difficult to obtain.

## 5.2 Basketball

- Player performance propositions.

## 5.3 Tennis

## 5.4 Cricket

# 6. Accumulator Research Laboratory

Accumulators deserve particular attention because their mathematical and practical characteristics differ significantly from those of individual predictions.

FoundrySide should investigate accumulators as a separate research discipline rather than treating them as a simple multiplication of individual selection probabilities.

The system should investigate:

- Different accumulator sizes.
- Same-match combinations.
- Cross-match combinations.
- Different market combinations.
- Combinations involving different sports.
- The effect of adding or removing individual selections.
- The relationship between combined odds and actual probability.
- The effect of correlation between selections.

An especially important issue is dependence between selections.

For example, both teams scoring and over 2.5 goals in the same football match are related outcomes. Their probabilities cannot safely be multiplied as though they were independent.

The system must investigate appropriate methods for estimating joint probabilities and correlations.

Accumulator evaluation should distinguish between:

1. Individual selection accuracy.
2. Probability of the complete accumulator succeeding.
3. Fair combined odds.
4. Bookmaker-implied probability.
5. Expected value.
6. Variance and volatility.
7. Probability of losing the entire stake.
8. Losing streaks.
9. Performance across different accumulator sizes.
10. Performance across different selection strategies.

The objective is to understand which combinations, if any, provide statistically defensible advantages, and under what conditions.

A successful individual prediction model does not automatically imply a successful accumulator strategy.

# 7. Jackpot Research Laboratory

Jackpots should be treated as a distinct research area.

Their evaluation differs from ordinary individual predictions because success often depends on correctly predicting a large number of fixtures within one entry.

FoundrySide should investigate available jackpots, their structures and the possibility of conducting simulated entries.

The system should aim to record:

- Jackpot operator.
- Competition or event.
- Number of fixtures.
- Fixture list.
- Entry cost.
- Prize structure.
- Rules and scoring conditions.
- Closing deadline.
- Available odds or other relevant information.
- Final results.

Where possible, the system should preserve the exact fixture list and rules associated with each jackpot.

Different predictive engines should be allowed to generate independent simulated entries.

Their results should be evaluated against actual jackpot outcomes.

Useful measurements include:

- Number of correctly predicted fixtures.
- Frequency of reaching specified success thresholds.
- Frequency of near-perfect entries.
- Frequency of perfect entries where applicable.
- Performance by prediction engine.
- Performance by competition and market.
- Performance across different jackpot structures.

All predictions must be frozen before the relevant events begin.

The system must preserve unsuccessful entries and avoid retrospective changes.

A model that performs well on individual matches may still perform poorly when evaluated against an entire jackpot. This distinction must remain visible.

# 8. Different User Profiles and Decision-Making Scenarios

FoundrySide should investigate how the same predictive information serves users with different expectations, budgets and attitudes towards risk.

The platform should not assume that every user has the same objective.

The following profiles are initial research scenarios, not fixed user categories.

## 8.1 High-variance, small-stake profile

A user might stake approximately KSh 30–100 and be interested in combinations that offer relatively large potential returns.

The user may be relatively comfortable losing a small stake, but that does not mean losses are irrelevant.

Research should investigate:

- Probability of large accumulator wins.
- Frequency of losing entries.
- Accumulator size.
- Probability of total loss.
- Expected value.
- Long-term performance.
- The frequency with which attractive returns are actually achieved.

The platform should distinguish between a high potential payout and a genuinely favourable probability of success.

## 8.2 Moderate-risk profile

A user might stake approximately KSh 100–300 and hope to achieve returns in the region of KSh 1,000–3,000.

Such a user may tolerate occasional losses but still cares about the frequency and size of unsuccessful entries.

Research should investigate:

- Different combination sizes.
- Probability of achieving the target return.
- Expected value.
- Losing streaks.
- Return frequency.
- Risk-adjusted comparisons between different strategies.

The system should not simply optimise for the largest possible payout.

## 8.3 Capital-preservation and high-loss-sensitivity profile

A user might stake KSh 500 or more and hope to double or triple the amount.

This user is particularly sensitive to losing the stake.

The system should investigate whether any available options offer a defensible balance between potential return and the probability of losing the investment.

Important measurements include:

- Probability of losing the full stake.
- Probability of achieving the intended return.
- Expected value.
- Drawdowns.
- Losing streaks.
- Variance.
- Historical reliability.

The platform should be able to communicate when available evidence does not justify placing a selection.

Sometimes the most useful recommendation may be not to bet.

These profiles should be investigated without implying guaranteed returns or encouraging users to chase losses.

# 9. Autonomous Operation

Autonomy is one of the defining requirements of FoundrySide.

The long-term objective is to reduce the amount of manual intervention required to operate the research ecosystem.

The user should not have to instruct the system to investigate every competition, run every experiment or evaluate every prediction.

Subject to technical feasibility, FoundrySide should eventually be able to:

1. Discover upcoming events.
2. Identify supported markets.
3. Collect available data.
4. Validate incoming information.
5. Generate predictions.
6. Record and freeze predictions.
7. Monitor relevant event developments.
8. Retrieve final results.
9. Settle predictions.
10. Evaluate model performance.
11. Identify prediction errors.
12. Generate new research hypotheses.
13. Run controlled experiments.
14. Compare experimental results.
15. Report meaningful findings.
16. Continue operating.

Autonomy should include the ability to recognise when additional information is needed, when a data source has failed and when an experiment cannot be trusted.

It should not mean allowing an automated system to silently change its methodology or rewrite its historical performance.

## 9.1 Event-driven scheduling

The system should not necessarily depend on one daily execution cycle.

Different activities may require different schedules.

For example:

- Fixture discovery could run periodically.
- Odds collection could be triggered by relevant market changes.
- Predictions could be generated at defined intervals.
- Results could be retrieved after events finish.
- Model evaluation could run after sufficient outcomes accumulate.
- Research agents could investigate identified weaknesses.
- Reports could be generated at appropriate intervals.

The research should establish which processes genuinely need frequent execution and which can operate less often.

The objective is to balance responsiveness, data freshness, reliability and resource consumption.

## 9.2 Resource-aware autonomy

Autonomous operation must be designed around realistic resource constraints.

The system should avoid repeatedly downloading unchanged information, making unnecessary API calls or using expensive AI inference for routine calculations.

Incremental collection, caching, scheduling and change detection should be investigated.

Conventional statistical and machine-learning models should handle high-volume numerical prediction wherever practical.

LLM-based agents should be used selectively for tasks where reasoning, research, hypothesis generation, interpretation or orchestration provides meaningful value.

# 10. Data Collection and Integrity

The quality of the entire ecosystem will depend heavily on the quality of its data.

FoundrySide should investigate data sources covering:

- Historical results.
- Fixtures and schedules.
- Team and player statistics.
- Standings and league tables.
- Injuries and suspensions.
- Squad information.
- Form and performance indicators.
- Match context.
- Bookmaker odds.
- Market movements.
- Jackpot information.
- Other relevant contextual information.

The research must distinguish between freely accessible information, paid data, restricted APIs and data that cannot legally or reliably be collected.

Each source should be evaluated according to:

- Sports and competitions covered.
- Historical depth.
- Update frequency.
- Accuracy and completeness.
- API limitations.
- Cost.
- Licensing and permitted usage.
- Reliability.
- Availability from Kenya.
- Suitability for automated collection.

Data provenance must be preserved.

The system should be able to distinguish between historical records of varying quality, identify missing information and record the source of each important observation.

Predictions must only use information that would genuinely have been available at the time the prediction was made.

This is essential to prevent data leakage and misleading backtesting results.

# 11. Performance Measurement and Scientific Evaluation

FoundrySide should not define success through headline accuracy alone.

Different measurements reveal different aspects of predictive performance.

Potential evaluation metrics include:

- Accuracy.
- Precision and recall where appropriate.
- Brier score.
- Log loss.
- Probability calibration.
- Expected value.
- Closing-line value.
- Return on investment in carefully controlled simulations.
- Drawdown.
- Variance.
- Losing streaks.
- Performance by market, sport, league and time period.

The research should determine which measurements are appropriate for each type of prediction.

For probability forecasts, calibration and proper scoring rules are particularly important.

A model that predicts an outcome with 80% confidence should, over a sufficiently large and representative sample, experience that outcome approximately 80% of the time.

The system should also compare itself against meaningful baselines.

These might include:

- Market-implied probabilities.
- Simple historical-frequency models.
- Basic team-strength models.
- Existing established statistical methods.
- Other independently developed engines.

Backtesting should use appropriate chronological separation between training, validation and testing data.

The system should investigate sample size, selection bias, overfitting, changing market conditions and statistical uncertainty.

No model should be declared superior simply because it performs well on a small or selectively chosen sample.

# 12. International Market and Data Provider Research

FoundrySide should investigate the international sports betting ecosystem, with particular attention to data accessibility and practical availability from Kenya.

The research must distinguish between three different categories.

**First: Betting operators.** These are organisations that accept wagers. Their ability to accept customers from Kenya, applicable licensing requirements and terms must be investigated independently.

**Second: Data providers.** These provide sporting results, statistics, fixtures, odds and other information. Access to their data does not automatically provide the right to place bets with the bookmakers whose odds they report.

**Third: Betting exchanges.** These facilitate market participation through exchange mechanisms and may have different data access, account and regulatory requirements.

Potential organisations and services to investigate include:

- The Odds API.
- Odds-API.io.
- Betfair Exchange.
- Pinnacle.
- bet365.
- 1xBet.
- 22Bet.
- Other relevant international and regional providers.

These are research candidates, not endorsements or confirmed accessible services.

For each candidate, investigate:

- Whether the service is accessible from Kenya.
- Whether account registration is permitted.
- Whether the relevant API or website can be accessed reliably.
- Whether the service accepts Kenyan customers where applicable.
- Relevant licensing and regulatory restrictions.
- Data coverage.
- API limits.
- Historical data availability.
- Cost.
- Terms of service.
- Restrictions on automated collection and redistribution.

The project should initially concentrate on research, data collection and simulated evaluation rather than real-money betting execution.

Any future activity involving accepting wagers or operating a gambling service would require a separate legal and licensing assessment.

# 13. Existing Products, Research and Open-Source Alternatives

FoundrySide should not assume that its proposed capabilities are entirely new.

A comprehensive review should investigate existing commercial prediction products, open-source systems, academic research, statistical literature and relevant mathematical methods.

The research should establish:

- What already exists.
- What works.
- What does not work.
- What can be reproduced.
- What can be improved.
- What is impractical to reproduce.
- What would be unnecessarily expensive to build independently.

Potential research areas include:

- Sports forecasting systems.
- Football prediction models.
- Rating systems such as Elo-based approaches.
- Poisson-based goal models.
- Bayesian approaches.
- Machine-learning forecasting.
- Ensemble modelling.
- Market efficiency research.
- Probability calibration.
- Correlation modelling for accumulators.
- Portfolio and risk analysis.
- Jackpot optimisation and combinatorial approaches.

Existing methods should be evaluated on their merits.

FoundrySide should be willing to adopt established approaches where they are effective and investigate alternatives where evidence reveals limitations.

The objective is not to reinvent everything. It is to build a system that makes intelligent use of existing knowledge while contributing its own research.

# 14. Infrastructure and Zero-Cost Feasibility

A major project constraint is the desire to operate FoundrySide with minimal recurring expenditure.

The preferred target is KSh 0 in recurring infrastructure costs wherever technically possible.

If a completely free solution is not sustainable, the project should investigate the least expensive practical alternatives.

A modest recurring cost in the region of KSh 100–200 per month may be considered if it provides a substantial and necessary advantage, but this is not an automatic budget allocation.

The research should investigate:

- Free cloud hosting.
- Free databases.
- Serverless execution.
- Scheduled workloads.
- GitHub Actions.
- Cloudflare Workers.
- Supabase and comparable services.
- Student cloud credits.
- Existing infrastructure that could be reused.
- Local machines and user-controlled resources.
- Hybrid architectures.

Promotional credits must be distinguished from permanently free services.

A system that operates for several months on temporary credits but later requires substantial payment should not be described as a zero-cost solution.

The investigation must examine actual service limits, including:

- CPU.
- RAM.
- Storage.
- Database capacity.
- Bandwidth.
- Execution time.
- Request quotas.
- Scheduled-job limits.
- Network restrictions.
- Data retention.
- Availability and reliability.

The system should be designed to minimise resource consumption through efficient collection, caching, incremental processing and selective computation.

It should also investigate whether demanding research workloads can be separated from routine prediction operations.

# 15. Technical Architecture and Development Feasibility

No technology stack should be selected prematurely.

DeepSeek should independently investigate the most appropriate architecture based on the project's requirements and realistic infrastructure limitations.

Areas requiring investigation include:

- Backend architecture.
- Data ingestion and validation.
- Prediction engines.
- Statistical and machine-learning infrastructure.
- Database design.
- Historical experiment storage.
- Scheduling and orchestration.
- Autonomous research agents.
- User interface.
- API design.
- Security.
- Deployment.
- Monitoring.
- Backup and recovery.

The eventual product may require several independently deployable components rather than one monolithic application.

However, architectural complexity should be justified by actual requirements.

The system should be capable of evolving without requiring a complete redesign whenever another sport, market or prediction engine is introduced.

The user-facing product should eventually make complex research understandable without exposing users to unnecessary technical complexity.

# 16. Reporting and User Intelligence

FoundrySide should eventually communicate more than a list of predicted outcomes.

A useful prediction should be accompanied by relevant information about its underlying probability, uncertainty and supporting evidence.

Potential information includes:

- Predicted outcome.
- Estimated probability.
- Market-implied probability.
- Difference between the two.
- Available odds.
- Model or engine responsible.
- Relevant supporting factors.
- Confidence and uncertainty.
- Historical performance in comparable situations.
- Data-quality warnings.
- Explanation of significant limitations.

The system should distinguish between a model's confidence and the strength of the evidence supporting that confidence.

It should not present every numerical difference as a betting opportunity.

A selection with a seemingly favourable estimated probability may still be unsuitable because of uncertainty, missing data, poor market availability or an insufficient sample.

The platform should be comfortable communicating that no defensible conclusion can be reached.

# 17. Safety, Transparency and Responsible Decision Support

FoundrySide must avoid misleading users.

It should not promise guaranteed wins, fixed returns or risk-free betting.

It should not conceal losing predictions, manipulate historical performance or present simulated results as real-world results.

Risk information should be visible, particularly for accumulator and jackpot scenarios.

The platform should be able to communicate:

- The probability of losing.
- The possibility of prolonged losing streaks.
- The uncertainty of estimated probabilities.
- The difference between potential payout and expected value.
- The limitations of historical backtesting.
- When available evidence is insufficient.

Real-money execution is outside the initial research scope.

Any future proposal to add such functionality must be considered separately, including regulatory, operational and responsible-gambling implications.

# 20. Project Constraints and Guiding Principles

The following principles should guide the investigation.

**Research before implementation.** No application development should begin merely because a concept sounds feasible.

**Evidence before promotion.** Predictive methods should demonstrate improvements through appropriate testing.

**Preserve history.** Predictions, experiments, failures and model versions should remain auditable.

**Independent approaches.** Competing methods should be allowed to operate and be evaluated separately.

**Resource efficiency.** Minimise unnecessary API requests, compute, storage and AI inference.

**Autonomy with accountability.** Automated processes should be able to work independently while preserving their decisions and results.

**Broad investigation, selective implementation.** Investigate opportunities widely, but implement only what is justified.

**No guaranteed outcomes.** Statistical predictions must never be presented as certainties.

**No premature architectural commitment.** Technology choices must follow the findings of the research.

**Practical accessibility.** Consider the actual cost, availability and restrictions of operating from Kenya.

**Continuous improvement.** The project should be designed to investigate and improve its own predictive performance over time.

# 22. From Pre-Proposal to Formal Proposal

This document is deliberately not the final proposal.

It establishes the questions that must be answered before FoundrySide can be designed responsibly.

The next phase should follow this sequence:

**Stage 1 — Establish the research foundation**

Use this pre-proposal as the shared reference for the investigation. Identify missing questions and clarify any ambiguous requirements.

**Stage 2 — Conduct independent investigations**

ChatGPT and DeepSeek should investigate the relevant areas, challenge assumptions and contribute findings.

DeepSeek should provide its own technical and feasibility assessment rather than merely endorsing the existing vision.

**Stage 3 — Compare and challenge findings**

Compare the research conclusions, investigate disagreements and identify claims that lack sufficient evidence.

Where necessary, conduct additional research or technical tests.

**Stage 4 — Establish the realistic project scope**

Determine which capabilities are feasible, which should be prioritised, which depend on future resources and which should be excluded.

**Stage 5 — Produce the formal proposal**

Only after the research has been sufficiently examined should the project produce a formal proposal containing:

- Final objectives.
- Defined scope.
- Proposed architecture.
- Technology stack.
- Infrastructure plan.
- Data strategy.
- Development phases.
- Estimated costs.
- Development timeline.

- Success criteria.

**Stage 6 — Obtain development authorisation**

The formal proposal must be reviewed before implementation begins.

Until that point, the project remains in the research and planning phase.

# 23. Final Statement of Intent

FoundrySide is an attempt to build something more ambitious than a conventional sports prediction application.

It is an attempt to establish an autonomous research environment capable of continuously investigating sporting events, understanding markets, testing mathematical approaches, evaluating predictions and discovering better methods.

The project should not be limited by the assumption that existing prediction methods are already sufficient. Nor should it assume that every unconventional idea will work.

Its strength should come from the willingness to investigate, measure, challenge and improve.

Even a small but genuine improvement in predictive performance can be meaningful if it is repeatable, independently verified and achieved without compromising reliability.

Equally, discovering that a promising approach does not work is a valuable result if the experiment explains why.

The ultimate objective is to give users better information, more accurate assessments of uncertainty and a clearer understanding of the decisions they face.

**FoundrySide should be built on evidence, not promises; experimentation, not assumptions; and measurable improvement, not appearances.**

The immediate task is therefore not to build FoundrySide.

It is to establish, through rigorous and independent research, what FoundrySide should become, what it can realistically achieve and how it can be developed sustainably.
