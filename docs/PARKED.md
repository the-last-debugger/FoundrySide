# Parked research directions

Directions the sponsor has deliberately deferred. Recorded so they are not lost and not
mistaken for current work. Neither is authorised for build.

## C-01 — Contextual intelligence and anomaly discovery

**Status: parked.** Approved as a future research direction on 2026-11-03. Not a
priority, not scheduled, not to be built.

**The idea.** Connect the smaller details around a match: official and predicted lineups,
player availability, manager and tactical changes, stadium and environmental conditions,
press conferences, credible news, historical patterns, and unusual but fan-recognised
observations.

**Why it is parked rather than pursued.** The core instrument is unfinished. Opening a
contextual-data programme now would consume the attention and budget that the first
confirmatory experiment needs, and it would answer a question — "is any of this
informative?" — that cannot be evaluated cleanly until the measurement apparatus is
trusted.

**What is not authorised.** A news archive, a contextual database, an ingestion pipeline,
or a feature system. None of these may be built now, and none may divert T0/T1
follow-through or the design of the first confirmatory experiment.

**What is allowed now.** Occasional low-cost desk notes, only when directly relevant to
an active test, kept exploratory and separate from confirmatory work.

**Conditions for reopening.** At a later gate, when the core work has matured, with a
written case covering: data coverage, provenance, licensing, and — the one that decides
it — an incremental-value test showing what the context adds over the price and over the
core model.

**Point-in-time requirements if it is ever opened.** These are non-negotiable and are
recorded now so they cannot be quietly dropped later:

- event time versus the time the information became available;
- official facts versus rumours and fan speculation;
- predicted lineups versus confirmed lineups;
- pre-match information versus in-play and post-match information.

**No arbitrary weights.** A fan-recognised pattern is a hypothesis to test, not a signal
to assume. Any "the crowd noticed something" input enters as a claim with a test, in the
same schema as everything else, and is scored like everything else.
