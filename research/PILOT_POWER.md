## Monte Carlo stability of the decision statistic

Each replicate's power is an estimate from **400** simulated t-tests, and
the 5th percentile of 2,000 such estimates is itself a noisy quantity — and it is exactly
where the 0.80 rule is applied, so its noise has to be measured rather than assumed.

## What this shows, and what it does not

**Shows:** the confirmatory design reaches the power bar at the measured spread of this model's Δ, with the uncertainty in the variance components inside the answer — not just at their point estimates.

**Does not show:** that a *competitive* model has the same spread. This model is worse than the market by a measurable margin, and its Δ variance is the input; a better model would typically have smaller variance, which makes this estimate conservative rather than flattering. It also does not make the effect size real — 0.01 nats remains a target, not a measurement.

## Why the 5th percentile rather than the median

If the design reaches 80% power at the median estimate of the variance components, it is
below 80% for half the plausible values of those components. Requiring the lower tail to
clear the bar makes the decision conservative in the direction that matters: a design
that fails this test may still work, but a design that passes it is not relying on a
favourable draw.
